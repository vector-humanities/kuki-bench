# Fixes 2a: Chapter 2, sections 1 to 7 (pp. 47-93)

### p. 50 | CHANGES
OLD: I have simply followed Lotze, who says that
NEW: We have simply followed Lotze, who says that
WHY: Literal "Here we have simply followed Lotze"; 我々 is "we" by the house convention, and the readable's own previous sentence keeps "we cannot go deeply into it here".

### p. 50 | CHANGES
OLD: In line with this I distinguish the hypothetical relation into three, groundedness, causality and purposiveness,
NEW: We have distinguished the hypothetical relation into three, groundedness, causality and purposiveness,
WHY: Literal "We have distinguished the hypothetical relation into the three of groundedness, causality and purposiveness"; the readable turns "we" into "I" and adds the link "In line with this".

### p. 50 | CHANGES
OLD: causal necessity and teleological necessity, I separate out three kinds of contingency: contingency of ground,
NEW: causal necessity and teleological necessity, have separated out three kinds of contingency: contingency of ground,
WHY: Literal "and ... have separated the three of contingency of ground, causal contingency and teleological contingency"; same "we" subject as the preceding fix.

### p. 63 | CHANGES
OLD: The essence of the pivot word, we might note, is to split one sound into two different meanings
NEW: The essence of the pivot word, further, is to split one sound into two different meanings
WHY: Literal "Further, the essence of the pivot word is to split one sound"; "we might note" turns Kuki's statement into a tentative aside.

### p. 68 | CHANGES
OLD: it can take on actuality and act as a cause only through the fact that consciousness anticipates time.
NEW: it can take on actuality and act as a cause only through the fact of the temporal anticipation of consciousness.
WHY: Literal "only through the fact of the temporal anticipation of consciousness"; consciousness anticipates in time (the future), it does not "anticipate time".

### p. 72 | ADDS
OLD: insofar as it can be thought to be related to *fui to*, "unawares", there should be no objection
NEW: insofar as it can be thought to be related to *fui to*, there should be no objection
WHY: Literal "insofar as it can be thought to be related to *fui to* (不意と)"; the gloss "unawares" is the translator's, and footnote c2s5-1 already gives it.

### p. 74 | CHANGES
OLD: In representing the relation between p and q by ○, Lewis shows that whether things will turn out p or q is left open as a problem, undetermined.
NEW: That the relation between p and q is represented by ○ shows that whether things will turn out p or q is left open as a problem, undetermined.
WHY: Literal "That the relation between p and q is expressed by ○ shows that ..."; the reading of ○ as an open problem is Kuki's, and the readable attributes it to Lewis.

### p. 75 | CHANGES
OLD: When Christian theology explains everything by the will of God and recognises the existence of miracles, which signify causal contingency, it rests on this relation.
NEW: When Christian theology explains everything by the will of God, its recognition of the existence of miracles, which signify causal contingency, rests on this relation.
WHY: Literal "its recognition of the existence of miracles ... rests on this relation"; in the readable the whole theological explanation, not the recognition of miracles, rests on the relation.

### p. 83 | CHANGES
OLD: In elucidating contingency, I try to proceed step by step from the subjective towards the objective. I began by treating categorical contingency
NEW: In elucidating contingency, we try to proceed step by step from the subjective towards the objective. We began by treating categorical contingency
WHY: Literal "We try ... First of all we treated ... Next we treated"; 我々 is "we", and the readable's next sentence returns to "we" ("Now that we leave").

### p. 89 | CHANGES
OLD: and ever since Boethius commented on it (Boethius, De interpretatione, Ed. II. lib. III. Migne p. 491, 506) it has been cited again and again as an example of chance.
NEW: and Boethius later commented on it (Boethius, De interpretatione, Ed. II. lib. III. Migne p. 491, 506), and ever since it has often been cited as an example of chance.
WHY: Literal "has often been cited as an example of chance ever since Aristotle gave it ... and Boethius later commented on it"; the readable dates the tradition from Boethius alone.

### p. 91 | ADDS
OLD: so that it is as if a notice read 'AK literary and arts department closed today'.
NEW: so that it takes the form of 'AK literary and arts department closed today'.
WHY: Literal "so that it takes the form of 'AK literary and arts department closed today'"; the notice is the translator's image, inside a newspaper quotation.

## Literal problems

None found. The citation "Aristoteles, *Metaphysica*, Δ. 7. 1072b" (p. 70) is Kuki's own error for Λ 7; the literal reproduces it correctly and readable footnote c2s4-7 records it.

## Summary

ADDS 2; DROPS 0; CHANGES 9; RULE 0. Total 11 (the two p. 50 entries fix one sentence).

The readable version of pp. 47-93 follows the literal closely: Kuki's citations, page references and emphasis (italics for 圏点) all survive, no house-rule breaches were found in the body text, and the translator's footnote dates and identifications checked out. The faults are small and local, the most consequential being three passages where Kuki's "we" becomes "I" against the convention, the misreading of "temporal anticipation of consciousness" on p. 68, and the attribution to Lewis on p. 74 of a reading of ○ that is Kuki's.

Not entered as fixes: the readable gives the Man'yōshū, Kakyō hyōshiki and other poems on pp. 57 and 62-64 in romanisation or translation only and omits Kuki's Japanese text, consistently with its practice across the whole file; whether to restore the Japanese is a policy question.
