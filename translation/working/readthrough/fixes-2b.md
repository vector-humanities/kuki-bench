# Fixes 2b: Chapter 2, sections 8 to 15 (pp. 93-150)

### p. 94 | CHANGES
OLD: In positive teleological contingency, that is, a kind of "purpose without purpose" usually casts a strong shadow over events.
NEW: In positive teleological contingency in particular, a kind of "purpose without purpose" usually casts a strong shadow.
WHY: Literal "In positive teleological contingency, in particular" (特に); "that is" turns a restriction into a restatement, and "over events" is not in Kuki.

### p. 95 | DROPS
OLD: whilst positive teleological contingency places its stress on the second.[^c2s8-2]
NEW: whilst positive teleological contingency places its stress rather on the second.[^c2s8-2]
WHY: Literal "places its stress rather on the lower limb" (むしろ下肢に力点を置く).

### p. 96 | CHANGES
OLD: "With the rise of the question of a silver loan from America, intended to relieve the price of silver, and with a faction at home responding to it,
NEW: "With the rise of the question of a silver loan for the purpose of relieving the price of silver in America, and with a faction at home responding to it,
WHY: Literal "a silver loan for the purpose of relieving the price of silver in America" (アメリカの銀價救濟目的の銀借款); America is where silver prices are relieved, not the source of the loan.

### p. 96 | ADDS

WHY: Literal "In Mushanokōji Saneatsu's *His Sister*"; "play" is a gloss, already in note c2s8-5.

### p. 97 | ADDS
OLD: According to Piéron, if we are walking by a lake and there happens to be a boat there, we do not especially call this chance.
NEW: According to Piéron, if we are walking by a lake and there is a boat there, we do not especially call this chance.
WHY: Literal "there is a boat there" (そこにボートがあつても); "happens to be" writes chance into the case Piéron offers as not chance.

### p. 110 | CHANGES

WHY: Literal "and in the fourth example something that ought to exist is asserted not to exist, and by this counter-purposiveness is pointed out"; counter-purpose belongs to the fourth example (p. 109), not to the tripod, stone and horse.

WHY: Literal "Further, in Eastern thought there is a marked tendency"; "we might note" is an aside not in Kuki.

WHY: Literal "And theorists who stress how far-reaching its philosophical outlook is have gradually increased in number"; "accordingly" adds a causal link and "gradually" is dropped.

## Literal problems

- p. 110: the literal's sentence "In the first, second and third examples something that ought not to exist is asserted not to exist, and in the fourth example ... and by this counter-purposiveness is pointed out" is correct to the image (コマ 62, right page: 第一第二第三の各例では無い筈のものが無いと斷定され、第四の例では有る筈のものが無いと斷定されることによつて反目的性が指摘されてゐるのである). The によつて clause can be read as governing both halves; the context (counter-purpose is the fourth example only, p. 109) favours the literal's reading, on which the readable fix above rests.
- p. 96: the image (コマ 55) confirms the literal on the silver loan: アメリカの銀價救濟目的の銀借款問題.
- No other errors found in the literal for this range.

## Summary

ADDS 4; DROPS 2; CHANGES 4; RULE 0. Total 10.

The readable version of sections 8 to 15 follows the literal closely, with no house-rule breaches (no dashes, banned words or aphoristic closers) and Kuki's emphasis carried through. The faults are local: one misattribution of the silver loan to America as lender (p. 96), one reattribution of counter-purposiveness to the first three *automaton* examples (p. 110), and a handful of small added asides or connectives.
